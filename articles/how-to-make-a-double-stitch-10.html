How to Make a Double Crochet Stitch

Build a US double crochet with two pull-throughs after drawing up a loop.

By Ariane Moreira

A US double crochet (dc) is taller than single or half double crochet. Its height comes from the yarn-over before insertion and two separate pull-throughs.

Before you begin

Confirm the pattern uses US terms. In UK patterns, “dc” names a shorter stitch, so a US tutorial will not match those instructions.

Step-by-step instructions

  1. Yarn over once, insert the hook, yarn over, and pull up a loop. Three loops are on the hook.
  2. Yarn over and pull through the first two loops. Two remain.
  3. Yarn over again and pull through the remaining two loops. One active loop remains.
  4. Make the next stitch in the next indicated stitch, not the same point unless the pattern says to increase.

What to check

Separate the two pull-throughs

The post shows two stacked sections and the top forms a V. If only one pull-through stage was completed, the stitch may be too short.

Frequently asked questions

How many yarn overs happen before inserting?

One for a standard US double crochet. Later yarn overs complete the pull-throughs.

Why are my stitches different heights?

Check that both pull-through stages are complete and that you are not tightening the yarn unevenly.

Does the turning chain count as a double crochet?

It depends on the pattern. Check its turning-chain note and use the same rule at both row edges.

Why does my double crochet have a gap at the base?

The hook may have entered the wrong place or the stitch may be too loose for the fabric. Check the pattern’s insertion point and compare several stitches before changing your tension.

Follow the loop count through both pull-throughs

After drawing up a loop, three loops sit on the hook. The first yarn-over passes through the first two, leaving two; the next passes through those final two, leaving one active loop. Pause between stages while learning. Pulling through all three at once creates a different stitch, while skipping a stage makes the post shorter. Keep the working strand loose enough to pass through each pair without stretching the stitch below.

Keep the post and top V distinct

The post is the vertical body of a double crochet; the top V is where the next row often goes. A long post can lean while the fabric is held, so inspect the stitch when the swatch rests flat. If the row count changes, check whether the top V was skipped or whether two stitches were worked into one place. In US terms, a double crochet is taller than a half double crochet; a pattern’s turning-chain rule may affect the edge height.

Place the hook under the stitch top

On a later row, the pattern commonly directs the hook beneath both strands of the top V, but it may specify a front-loop or back-loop texture instead. Enter the named location once and avoid catching the adjacent stitch. At a foundation chain, follow the stated chain number from the hook. A consistent insertion point keeps the posts aligned and makes the top Vs easier to count.

Use row height and gauge as separate checks

The two pull-throughs create the stitch, while yarn, hook size, and tension determine how tall and wide it becomes. Make a swatch if the project’s size matters and compare the stitch and row gauge with the pattern. If the row is too tall, first check that you completed the same stitch sequence and are not drawing up unusually long loops. Do not replace double crochet with a different stitch to adjust height unless the pattern allows the substitution.

Check the edge before counting the row

At the start and end of a double-crochet row, the turning chain can affect the apparent height and stitch total. Read whether the chain counts and where the first stitch belongs. If the edge looks like a gap, do not automatically put an extra stitch into it; compare the written edge instruction and the row count. Marking the first true stitch can help while learning. Once the edge matches the pattern, count the completed top Vs across the row and compare the total before turning again.

Common mistakes and how to fix them

  • Pulling through all loops in one motion. A standard US double crochet finishes through two pairs, not one group of three. Pause after the first pair.
  • Skipping the second yarn-over. That leaves the stitch unfinished or at the wrong height. Check that one active loop remains after the second pull-through.
  • Confusing the top V with the post. Insert at the location named by the pattern; the top V and vertical post serve different visual purposes.

Tips that make it easier

  • Count “three, two, one” loops as you practice the two pull-throughs.
  • Hold the fabric below the stitch so the post stays visible without pulling it taut.
  • Check the pattern’s US or UK terms before using the abbreviation dc.

A useful next step

Check the pattern’s instructions against a short sample before continuing, then follow its stated stitch and edge rules.
