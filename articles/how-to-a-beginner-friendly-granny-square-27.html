How to Crochet a Beginner-Friendly Granny Square

Build a classic granny square by placing groups of US double crochet around a center and working corner spaces consistently.

By Ariane Moreira

Granny-square patterns vary in starting ring, cluster size, and corner chains. This describes a common construction; follow a specific pattern’s counts and joins when using one.

Before you begin

You need yarn, a matching hook, and a way to mark the start if the round is hard to see. The instructions assume US double crochet (dc).

Step-by-step instructions

  1. For one common version, chain 3 (counts as a US dc) and work 2 dc into a center ring; chain 2 for a corner. Repeat 3 dc and chain 2 three more times, then join to the top of the starting chain. Pattern starts and joins vary.
  2. Join the round if directed and move to a corner space. In a common next round, work 3 dc, chain 2, and 3 dc in each corner; work chain 1 between corner groups.
  3. On later rounds, work the specified corner groups into each corner space and a 3-dc cluster into each side space. Follow the pattern’s chain counts and repeats.
  4. Continue outward, keeping corners above corners and side clusters in matching spaces. Fasten off and secure tails.

What to check

Corners create the square

The diagram shows layout, but starting chains and joins vary. Count corner spaces after each round; the square should expand at the corners rather than curl sharply.

Frequently asked questions

How many chains go in each corner?

It depends on the pattern. Many use a corner chain space, but the number and placement vary.

Why is my square cupping?

Check that corners and side spaces match the pattern. Tension also affects shape; do not add stitches at random.

Do all granny squares use four chain-two corners?

No. The center, cluster count, corner chains, and join vary by pattern. The steps here describe one common version; use the counts in your chosen pattern.

Keep the corner spaces above one another

In the common version shown, each corner has a chain space where the next round places its corner group. Put the corner group in the existing corner space rather than in a nearby side space. Side clusters go between corners according to the pattern. If corners drift or the motif begins to twist, count the corner spaces and check where each cluster was worked before changing the stitch count.

Read the first round and join as a unit

The example begins with a chain counted as a US double crochet, then makes two more double crochets in the center and a chain-space corner. Other patterns may use a different starting chain, ring, or join. When the round closes, follow the instructions for moving to a corner space and beginning the next round. A slip-stitch join may not count as a stitch. Keep the pattern’s count and starting-chain rule beside you rather than assuming another square uses the same convention.

Know what can vary between square patterns

Some granny squares use solid rounds, different cluster sizes, or no chain between side clusters. Color changes and joining squares introduce additional finishing choices. The diagram here shows the layout for the written sample, not every granny-square design. If you plan to join several motifs, make more than one sample and compare their dimensions before joining. Blocking may help align a motif when the fiber allows it, but it does not replace matching counts and construction.

Common mistakes and how to fix them

  • Working into a side space as if it were a corner. Check the pattern and locate the corner chain space before placing the corner group.
  • Losing the round start. Use a marker or the stated join to keep track of the first stitch.
  • Assuming the starting chain has one universal count. Patterns differ on whether it counts as a stitch. Follow the written rule.

Tips that make it easier

  • Count four corner spaces after each round in this four-corner sample.
  • Use a marker in the first corner space if clusters look alike.
  • Compare motifs after the same number of rounds before joining them.

A useful next step

Check the pattern’s steps, materials, and fit on a small sample before committing to the full project. Use the written counts and care instructions for the yarn you choose.
