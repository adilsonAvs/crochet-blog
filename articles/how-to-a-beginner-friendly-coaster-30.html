How to Crochet a Beginner-Friendly Coaster

Shape a flat circular coaster by placing increases evenly around each round and checking for curling.

A circle needs increases to stay flat. This sample uses six evenly spaced increases per round in US sc; counts guide construction, not finished size.

Before you begin

Use a smooth yarn and suitable hook. Decide whether to join rounds or work in a spiral, then keep the method consistent.

Step-by-step instructions

  1. Make a starting ring and work six US sc into it for round one.
  2. For a common flat-circle sequence, increase in every stitch on round two to reach 12 stitches.
  3. On round three, place six increases evenly with one sc between them for 18 stitches; on round four, use two sc between increases for 24.
  4. Continue the increase logic, moving a marker at the round start. Fasten off at your preferred diameter.

What to check

Observe the fabric before adding rounds

Counts 6, 12, 18, and 24 follow this six-increase sample. If it cups, it may need more circumference; if it ripples, check increase count and tension before changing the pattern.

Frequently asked questions

Should every coaster have 24 stitches in round four?

No. That count follows this sample. Other stitch patterns or starting rings use different counts.

Why is the circle wavy?

It may have more circumference than needed. Check increase placement and tension before removing stitches.

Follow the coaster’s increase sequence

For beginner coaster, use this as the starting rule: follow the pattern’s shape, stitch count, and turning or round instructions. The stitch repeat, gauge, and sizing notes affect the finished shape and amount of work. Read the relevant instructions before beginning and keep them nearby as you work. If a general tutorial and the pattern give different directions, follow the pattern’s stated convention; another project may use a different method.

Check whether it is worked in rounds

Before proceeding, confirm whether the design is worked in rows, joined rounds, or a spiral. Write any project-specific choice in your notes so you can apply it consistently. If the pattern or label leaves the detail unclear, look for its own key or maker guidance rather than assuming that another project follows the same convention. This short check can prevent a small misunderstanding from affecting the rest of the work.

Use a marker at the round start

A practical way to apply beginner coaster: Read the pattern’s materials, repeat, size, and finishing notes, then set the work flat periodically and compare the pattern’s stitch progression. If the stitch or fit is unfamiliar, make a small sample and compare its texture and shape with the pattern’s description before committing to many rows or rounds. Keep the sizing and care information with the project as it grows.

Look for cupping or rippling

A common mistake in beginner coaster is adding stitches to force a piece flat without checking the instructions. Watch for this sign: edges ripple or cup as the shape develops. That can change the size, shape, or fit from what the pattern describes. Compare the recent work with the pattern and locate the first point where they differ. Correct that detail before continuing; stretching the fabric or adding an unplanned step can hide the cause without fixing it.

Adjust counts only when the pattern calls for it

If edges ripple or cup as the shape develops, first check whether the design is worked in rows, joined rounds, or a spiral. Then compare the stitch count, repeat, sizing note, and construction method with what you actually have. Change one thing at a time so you can tell whether it addresses the problem. If the instructions and materials match but the result still differs, use the project’s gauge, sizing, substitution, or care notes as relevant; those are more useful than a universal fix.

Consider yarn and hook together

Remember: troubleshoot by checking counts and construction before blocking. A sample reveals how the chosen yarn and stitch behave before you repeat them across a larger piece. If you choose an alternative, record it and compare a small sample or written step before applying the change across the project.

Check the finished shape before blocking

To make beginner coaster easier to repeat, use this sequence: set the work flat periodically and compare the pattern’s stitch progression, inspect the result, and only then continue. At first, slow down at the point where the pattern changes direction, begins a new round, or starts a repeat. A removable marker or brief row note can hold your place while you learn the construction. Once the sequence is clear, you can work more steadily without guessing which loop or instruction comes next.

Conclusion: Beginner coaster

Keep this lesson in mind: troubleshoot by checking counts and construction before blocking. Use the pattern’s gauge, repeat, and sizing notes as you continue. A coaster that cups or ripples may need a count or tension check. Blocking can shape the fabric, but it does not correct an incorrect increase sequence.
