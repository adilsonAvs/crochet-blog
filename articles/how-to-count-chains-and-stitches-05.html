How to Count Crochet Chains and Stitches

Count chain links and completed stitch tops without counting the active loop or a turning chain incorrectly.

By Ariane Moreira

A reliable count starts by identifying what the pattern means by “stitch.” Completed row stitches are counted across the row; chain spaces and turning chains depend on the instructions.

Before you begin

Lay the work flat in good light. If the edge loops are difficult to distinguish, place a removable marker in the first or last stitch.

Step-by-step instructions

  1. For a foundation chain, find the repeated V-shaped links. Do not count the loop currently on the hook.
  2. For a worked row, follow the top Vs one at a time from edge to edge.
  3. Check the pattern’s note about the turning chain; some patterns count it and others do not.
  4. Compare your total with the stated row count and inspect whether a chain space is being mistaken for a stitch.

What to check

Count the structure the pattern names

An off-by-one count often comes from the turning chain or an edge stitch. Count again from the opposite edge and compare your result with the pattern’s rule.

Frequently asked questions

Do I count the loop on my hook?

No. It is the active loop, not a completed chain or stitch.

Do I count a turning chain?

Follow the pattern’s stitch notes. The rule varies by stitch and pattern.

Do chain spaces count as stitches?

Only when the pattern tells you to count or work into them as stitches. Keep openings separate from completed stitch tops while counting.

Tell chains, stitches, and spaces apart

A chain is a linked row of V shapes. A completed stitch has a top V across the fabric, while a chain space is an opening created by one or more chains. The pattern may tell you to work into that opening, but the opening itself does not automatically add a stitch to the row total. The active loop stays on the hook and is excluded from either count. For a foundation chain, count completed links from the hook toward the tail. For a worked row, follow the stitch tops from one edge to the other.

Use row totals to locate a missed stitch

Compare your count with the total written for that row, then compare the edges with the previous row. If the number is short, inspect the last stitch before the point where the count changed; an edge stitch or a stitch hidden beside a turning chain is easy to miss. If the number is high, check whether a turning chain was counted or a space was mistaken for a stitch. For repeats, count the stitches in one repeat before counting the whole row. This narrows the search instead of requiring you to undo the entire row.

Count repeats, increases, and decreases

A repeat is a group of instructions, not one stitch. If a pattern says to repeat a sequence a set number of times, count the stitches made by one complete sequence before multiplying it across the row. Increases usually place more than one stitch in a location, while decreases combine stitches; follow the pattern’s definitions and count the completed stitches it says the row should contain. Do not count the number of hook insertions as the row total when a decrease combines them or an increase creates several stitches in one place. Check the stitch count at the end of each repeat if the row is long.

Common mistakes and how to fix them

  • Counting the loop on the hook. It is active, not completed. Begin with the first finished chain or stitch below it.
  • Mistaking a chain space for a stitch. A space is an opening, not a stitch top. Follow the pattern’s wording and count the structure it names.
  • Adding a turning chain by habit. Patterns differ on whether it counts. Check the row note before settling the edge count.

Tips that make it easier

  • Count in small groups and move a marker as you finish each group.
  • For a row, follow the top Vs and recount from the opposite edge if the total is off by one.
  • Write the expected stitch total beside rows with increases, decreases, or repeats.

A practical next step

Count in small groups and move a marker as you finish each group. Then check your result against the pattern before continuing.
