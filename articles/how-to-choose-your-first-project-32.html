How to Choose Your First Crochet Project

Choose a first crochet project with a few techniques, accessible materials, and a result you want to make.

By Ariane Moreira

A good first project leaves room to learn without requiring many new skills at once. The simplest choice depends on your interests and pattern support.

Before you begin

Check skill level, abbreviations, materials, dimensions, and whether the instructions explain row or round counts.

Step-by-step instructions

  1. Pick an idea such as a swatch-based scarf, small cloth, or motif that uses techniques you know.
  2. Read the full pattern and note unfamiliar stitches or construction steps before buying materials.
  3. Check yarn, hook, care needs, and whether the project requires sewing or shaping.
  4. Choose something easy to pause and resume. Make a practice swatch if a stitch or gauge is new.

What to check

Choose by technique, not dimensions

A small item may involve shaping or color changes; a larger rectangle can be simple if it repeats one stitch. Judge the instructions, not only finished size.

Frequently asked questions

Is a scarf always the easiest first project?

No. It may repeat one stitch but require many rows. A swatch or motif can be a shorter practice exercise.

Should I buy yarn before reading the pattern?

Read its materials and instructions first so you can choose a suitable yarn and amount.

Is a small project always easier?

No. A small motif can use shaping or color changes, while a larger rectangle may repeat one stitch. Read the steps and materials before judging by dimensions.

Compare project types by the skills they use

A simple rectangle can practice chains, rows, turning, and edge counts. A dishcloth or coaster introduces a useful small finish; a granny square adds rounds, corners, and joins; a headband adds fit and seaming. These are examples, not rankings. Read each pattern’s skill list and see whether it explains the techniques you do not know. Choose a project whose new steps feel manageable and interesting enough to keep practicing.

Check the pattern before buying supplies

Read the full materials list, abbreviations, stitch repeats, row or round counts, finishing, and care notes. Check whether there is a chart or written alternative and whether gauge or dimensions matter. A project with a photograph but brief instructions may leave more decisions to the maker than a longer, clearly explained pattern. Keep the yarn label and pattern together once you choose. If a step is unclear, look up that technique before beginning the whole project.

Plan for the time and finishing you want

A scarf can repeat a simple stitch but take many rows; a small motif may finish quickly but require shaping or sewing. Consider the time available, the number of techniques to learn, and whether you want to weave ends or join pieces. You can begin with a swatch or small sample before committing to a larger item. Finishing is part of the project, so read those instructions before deciding.

Use the comparison table as a starting point

The table summarizes skills that commonly appear in these project types, but patterns vary. One coaster can be a simple rectangle while another introduces rounds and increases; a scarf may use a repeat or color change. Open the actual instructions and look for unfamiliar terms, shaping, joins, and finishing. Choose a pattern that explains the steps you need or begin with a small swatch of the technique. A project is manageable when its instructions and materials make sense to you, not just because it is short.

Common mistakes and how to fix them

  • Choosing by finished size alone. Look at stitch changes, repeats, shaping, and finishing steps before choosing.
  • Starting before reading the abbreviations. Check the pattern key and unfamiliar terms so you can follow each line.
  • Buying yarn without checking the material list. Match the pattern’s suggested weight, amount, and care requirements where possible.

Tips that make it easier

  • Choose a project that introduces only a few new techniques at once.
  • Make a short swatch if stitch or gauge is unfamiliar.
  • Keep a row note or marker so it is easy to resume after a pause.

A useful next step

Check the pattern’s steps, materials, and fit on a small sample before committing to the full project. Use the written counts and care instructions for the yarn you choose.
