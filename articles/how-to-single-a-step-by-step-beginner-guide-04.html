How to Single Crochet: A Step-by-Step Beginner Guide

Learn the US single crochet by inserting the hook, pulling up a loop, and completing both loops together.

By Cozy Crochet Trail Editorial Team
On this page
  1. Before you begin
  2. Step-by-step instructions
  3. What to check
  4. FAQ

In US terminology, single crochet (sc) is a short, dense stitch. After insertion, pull up a loop, then draw yarn through both loops on the hook.

Before you begin

Use a foundation chain or small swatch and check the pattern’s turning-chain instructions. US and UK stitch names differ, so confirm the terminology first.

Step-by-step instructions

  1. Insert the hook into the stitch or chain specified. Keep the working yarn behind the work unless the pattern says otherwise.
  2. Yarn over and draw up a loop. Two loops should now be on the hook.
  3. Yarn over again and pull through both loops. One active loop remains.
  4. Repeat in the next stitch, entering each top V once rather than skipping an edge stitch.

What to check

Read the stitch top

A completed US single crochet has one active loop on the hook and a compact stitch below it. Look for a line of top Vs and count them if the edges begin to shift.

Frequently asked questions

How many loops are on the hook before the final pull-through?

Two: the active loop and the loop just pulled up.

Why does my single crochet look like a chain on top?

The top V is normal; its front and back loops form the two strands of the stitch top.
