How to Read Basic Crochet Pattern Abbreviations

Use common US crochet abbreviations and repeat symbols to read a pattern one instruction at a time.

By Cozy Crochet Trail Editorial Team
On this page
  1. Before you begin
  2. Step-by-step instructions
  3. How to check your work
  4. FAQ

Short answer: Crochet abbreviations shorten pattern instructions. In US patterns, sc means single crochet and ch means chain; read the pattern’s notes first because stitch terms and repeat symbols can vary by designer.

Before you begin

Keep the pattern’s notes, stitch key, and materials list nearby. The notes explain the designer’s terms, whether chains count as stitches, and how repeats are written.

This guide uses US crochet terms. Some UK terms use the same abbreviations for different stitches, so check whether the pattern identifies itself as US or UK terminology.

Step-by-step instructions

  1. Step 1: Read the pattern notes and stitch key before starting. Confirm whether it uses US terms and how it treats turning chains.
  2. Step 2: Learn the common short forms: ch = chain, sl st = slip stitch, sc = single crochet, st = stitch, sts = stitches, and rep = repeat.
  3. Step 3: Read punctuation as part of the instruction. Parentheses or brackets often group stitches; an asterisk usually marks a section to repeat. Check the pattern’s notes for its exact convention.
  4. Step 4: Translate one row into actions. For example, “ch 1, sc in next 3 sts” means make one chain, then one single crochet in each of the next three stitches. Mark repeats as you work.

How to check your work

After completing a row, compare your stitch count with the pattern’s stated total if one is provided. Make sure each repeat was worked the requested number of times.

If an abbreviation is not defined or its meaning is unclear, pause and check the designer’s key rather than guessing. Do not assume US and UK stitch names match.

Common beginner problems

  • If the finished row is too long or short, check whether you repeated the starred section the right number of times.
  • If instructions use parentheses or brackets, read the notes to see whether they group stitches to be made in one place or mark a repeat.
  • If sc or dc seems inconsistent with the tutorial, verify whether the pattern uses US or UK terms.

Read the pattern notes and stitch key first

The pattern’s notes define its abbreviations, repeat markers, and terminology. Check whether the designer uses US or UK terms before starting.

If an abbreviation is missing from the key, check the designer’s explanation rather than guessing from another pattern.

Practice plan

Take one row from a beginner pattern and underline each abbreviation. Expand them using the key, then work one instruction at a time.

Practice the movements with the US single-crochet lesson and review turning chains and row turns.

Quick summary

  • Read the designer’s notes and confirm US or UK terms.
  • Use the stitch key to expand abbreviations.
  • Follow repeat marks and punctuation exactly.

Remember: A pattern’s abbreviation key takes priority because designers can use different shorthand or repeat conventions.

Frequently asked questions

What does sc mean in a US crochet pattern?

Sc means single crochet in US terminology. Check the pattern’s notes if its terminology is not stated.

What do the asterisks mean?

An asterisk commonly marks the beginning of a repeat. Follow the pattern’s wording to learn where the repeat ends and how many times to work it.

Are US and UK abbreviations the same?

Some are, but stitch names and abbreviations can differ. Confirm which terminology the pattern uses before you begin.
