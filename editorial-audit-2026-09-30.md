# Editorial audit — Cozy Crochet Trail

**Date:** September 30, 2026  
**Scope:** All 40 article pages, their instructional images and diagrams, internal navigation and SEO metadata, plus the site's authorship and contact transparency pages.

## Overall findings

- **40 of 40 articles reviewed individually.** Five were adequate without editorial changes, 32 received small edits, and three needed more substantive instructional corrections.
- The clearest repeated-content issue was in articles 9–40: each had a `Short answer` paragraph that duplicated its deck word for word. Those 32 duplicate paragraphs were removed; the decks and article bodies remain.
- Three earlier articles also had a `Remember` paragraph repeating their own quick summary. Those redundant recaps were removed from articles 3, 5, and 8.
- Targeted corrections clarified a usable yarn-joining method (15), the side spaces in a granny square (27), completion of a coaster pattern (30), a gauge-based blanket-width example (31), and tension as a cause of width changes even when stitch counts match (36).
- The common sequence of practical sections—preparation, steps, checking, troubleshooting, and FAQs—helps beginners navigate the site. Reusing those headings is not itself evidence of low-value or policy-violating content, but future articles should keep their examples and explanations specific to the actual task.
- The dishcloth and washcloth guides (26, 28), hook-size and gauge guides (17, 21), and edge, first-stitch, and width-troubleshooting guides (8, 38, 36) overlap in subject matter but serve distinct purposes. No article was removed or merged.
- The five categories contain eight articles each. No category imbalance or clearly redundant article was identified.
- The article pages use one generic editorial-team byline. The Editorial Team page accurately says there is no named author, verified credential, or individual project testing. This is transparent, but it does not demonstrate first-hand expertise. A named author or reviewer should be added only if that person and review process are real and verifiable.
- Pexels photographs provide context; custom diagrams carry useful stitch-count, chart, gauge, and construction explanations. The visual assets are present, have alt text, and resolve to existing files. Photos are illustrative and are not presented as proof that a pattern was tested.
- The cover photo on the yarn-label article (20) shows crochet fabric rather than a label. Its accompanying diagram depicts label fields, so the teaching point is still represented; the existing image was preserved.

## Cross-site editorial and SEO findings

### Repetition and topical overlap

The most measurable repetition was the exact deck/answer echo in articles 9–40; it has been removed. The same section framework appears across the collection, but the steps and troubleshooting are topic-specific enough to orient readers. A future editorial pass should vary the opening and the worked example when there is a real difference in task, rather than varying wording just to appear less automated.

The articles were not labeled “AI-generated” based on style alone. Repeated prose is an observable editorial issue, but authorship cannot be reliably inferred from prose patterns. No automatic Google policy violation can be concluded from this audit. The key risk to monitor is publishing at scale without additional value; each article should continue to offer a distinct answer, useful steps, and suitable diagrams/photos.

### Images and accessibility

The audit found no missing article image files or missing image `alt` attributes. Cover images are contextual photographs of people crocheting; instructional diagrams are retained where stitch counts, gauge, chart direction, or construction benefit from a visual. Existing visuals were not replaced. The yarn-label cover photo is less specific than its diagram, which supplies the missing label details.

### SEO and navigation

All 40 articles have one H1, a title, a meta description, and a canonical URL. Internal article links, fragment targets, and article image paths resolve in the local project. Existing filenames were preserved to avoid breaking current links. A few slugs are less grammatical than their H1s (notably articles 4 and 26–28); these were not renamed because URL changes were outside this editorial pass and require redirect planning if the site is already published.

### Authorship and contact accuracy

The About and Editorial Team pages do not invent personal experience or credentials. The Contact page now has a Web3Forms form, but Privacy Policy and Terms of Use still described the site as having no form or working channel. Those stale statements were corrected: the privacy page now says that name, email, and message are sent to Web3Forms for processing and forwarding, links to the provider's policy, and notes its stated retention period; the terms page points readers to the Contact form. This is the minimum factual correction required after the earlier form integration.

The hidden email subject configured on the Contact form still says “Cozy Green Nook,” while the site is called Cozy Crochet Trail. It was left unchanged because that exact value was explicitly supplied in the earlier form request; the site owner may want to confirm it before relying on email notifications.

## Google guidance: policy vs. editorial practice

- **AdSense review guidance:** Google says a site should provide unique, relevant content and a good user experience, with clear navigation; its help page also lists insufficient content and content-quality issues as possible rejection reasons. Google does not provide a universal minimum word count or guarantee approval from any edit. [Prepare your site for AdSense](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/7299563?hl=en) · [Common content issues](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/81904?hl=en)
- **Google Search people-first guidance:** Google encourages complete, useful, original content, descriptive headings, accurate authorship, and content created primarily to help visitors. It explicitly asks site owners not to write to a preferred word count. [Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- **Policy distinction:** Google describes scaled content abuse as many pages produced primarily to manipulate search rather than help users, with little or no original value. The 40-page count or AI assistance alone is not proof of a violation. The repeated summary paragraphs were a quality and experience issue; removing them makes the text less repetitive, but cannot determine or guarantee a Google policy outcome. [Spam policies: scaled content abuse](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) · [Guidance on generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)
